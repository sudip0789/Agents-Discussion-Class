Further reading

In one line: the best short reads and videos for going deeper on anything from class, chosen for law students rather than engineers.

Nothing here is required. Each group starts with the most approachable item. All links were checked on October 12, 2026; if one has moved, search for its title.

How models work

  • Large Language Models explained briefly · 3Blue1Brown (video)

    An 8-minute visual introduction to how a chatbot predicts the next word. Start here.

  • Large Language Models explained briefly (text lesson) · 3Blue1Brown

    The written version of the video: pretraining, human feedback and transformers in plain English.

  • Transformers, the tech behind LLMs (Deep Learning, chapter 5) · 3Blue1Brown (video)

    A deeper visual tour of how text becomes numbers and then next-word probabilities.

  • The Illustrated Transformer · Jay Alammar

    A classic, diagram-heavy walkthrough of the “attention” design behind GPT and Claude. More technical, still readable.

  • Tiktokenizer · open-source tool

    Paste a paragraph and see exactly how it splits into tokens. Try a case citation or a Latin phrase.

  • Tokenizer · OpenAI

    OpenAI’s own version of the same tool.

  • What are tokens and how to count them? · OpenAI Help Center

    The source of the “roughly ¾ of a word” rule of thumb, with its caveats.

  • Glossary · Anthropic

    Short, plain definitions of tokens, context windows, temperature and other words used in class.

Agents and tools

  • Building effective agents · Anthropic (Erik Schluntz and Barry Zhang, Dec. 2024)

    The standard explainer on workflows versus agents and the tool-use loop. The source for lesson 2’s distinction.

  • How Claude Code works · Anthropic

    The agent loop (gather context, take action, verify), its tools, checkpoints and permissions.

  • Effective context engineering for AI agents · Anthropic (Sept. 2025)

    Why what sits in the model’s limited “working memory” matters, and how agents manage it.

  • Writing effective tools for agents — with agents · Anthropic (Sept. 2025)

    How the tools an agent can call are designed and tested.

  • Choose a permission mode · Anthropic

    What Manual, Edit automatically, Plan and Auto each allow, and how to switch between them.

  • Configure permissions · Anthropic

    Allow, ask and deny rules (deny always wins), how to write paths, and how to protect files like .env.

  • Subagents · Anthropic

    How to create helper agents with their own instructions and tool limits, like your case-briefer and brief-checker.

  • How Claude remembers your project · Anthropic

    Where CLAUDE.md and other standing instructions live, and when they load.

  • Model Context Protocol · MCP project

    The “USB-C port” standard for connecting AI apps to outside tools and data.

Safety and prompt injection

  • The lethal trifecta for AI agents · Simon Willison

    Why an agent with private data, untrusted content and a way to send messages out is dangerous. Short and memorable.

  • LLM01:2025 Prompt Injection · OWASP GenAI Security Project

    The security industry’s top-ranked AI risk, including “indirect” injection hidden in documents and web pages.

  • “Positive review only”: Researchers hide AI prompts in papers · Nikkei Asia (July 1, 2025)

    The real-world hidden-prompt case behind lesson 9’s prompt-injection test: 17 arXiv preprints aimed at AI peer reviewers.

  • Researchers embedded AI prompts in preprints to secure favourable reviews · Academica Group

    A short, open-access summary of the Nikkei story.

  • Prompt injection (tag archive) · Simon Willison

    More than 160 posts tracking real prompt-injection incidents as they happen.

Law and AI

  • Formal Opinion 512: Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools · ABA Standing Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility (July 29, 2024)

    The ABA’s guidance on competence, confidentiality, candor, supervision and fees when lawyers use AI.

  • Mata v. Avianca, Inc., Opinion and Order on Sanctions (S.D.N.Y. June 22, 2023) · CourtListener (RECAP)

    Judge Castel’s opinion sanctioning lawyers for filing cases ChatGPT invented. Worth reading in full.

  • Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools · Magesh et al., Stanford (J. Empirical Legal Stud. 2025)

    Even Lexis+ AI and Westlaw’s AI tools hallucinated on 17–33% of test queries. Also on arXiv.

  • AI on Trial: Legal Models Hallucinate in 1 out of 6 (or More) Benchmarking Queries · Stanford HAI

    A readable summary of the study above.

  • Large Legal Fictions: Profiling Legal Hallucinations in Large Language Models · Dahl, Magesh, Suzgun and Ho (J. Legal Analysis 2024)

    General chatbots got verifiable case-law questions wrong 58–88% of the time.

  • AI Hallucination Cases Database · Damien Charlotin

    A running list of court decisions about AI-hallucinated filings, more than 2,000 at our last check.

  • CodeX: The Stanford Center for Legal Informatics · Stanford Law School

    Stanford’s center for legal technology and computational law, if you want to keep going.

APIs and databases

  • Citation Lookup API · Free Law Project (CourtListener)

    The API your lookup.py uses in lesson 10 to check whether a citation is real. Needs a free token.

  • CourtListener REST API v4 overview · Free Law Project

    How to send your token and what the rate limits are.

  • arXiv API User’s Manual · arXiv

    Free and no key needed; the docs ask you to wait about three seconds between calls.

  • Crossref REST API · Crossref

    Free DOI and article lookup with no sign-up.

  • Semantic Scholar API · Allen Institute for AI

    Paper search with citation links. Works without a key, but is often busy; a free key helps.

  • Federal Register API · Office of the Federal Register

    Search proposed and final rules and notices. Free, no key.

  • eCFR API · Office of the Federal Register

    The current Code of Federal Regulations and its past versions. Free, no key.
